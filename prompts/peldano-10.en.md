## Prompt 1

Every pending item that changes what a user sees or does is closed with its manual test cases written in `docs/MANUAL_TESTS.md`, derived from its acceptance criteria: one case per flow the item touches, with an id, the pending item, the precondition, the numbered steps (what the person does and what they must see), the expected result including a backend effect a person can verify without tools, the test data to use, and an empty execution table (date, who, passed or failed, observation). Write them for a person who does not know how the system is built. Keep the cases of closed items up to date: when a flow changes, its cases change in the same commit. Never mark a case as executed yourself: execution is human. Every manual case also runs once per affected profile, and it is derived from `docs/PROFILE_MATRIX.md` instead of being written by hand: a case run with a single profile is not an executed case (Annex H, anchor 4). At the close of each block add the two human checks no machine replaces: the first-click check, where I name the place I would look for a task and you compare that with the menu, and the real screenshot of the test environment. `docs/MANUAL_TESTS.md` carries at its head three declarations that a gate reads, each one under its fixed key in English even when the file is in my language, because what this step owes is not the same as what it can prove. `cases_written`: how many cases are written. `cases_executed_by_a_person`: how many of those a person signed in their execution table, with a date and a name; you keep the first figure, and the second one moves only when a person executes -- never from your own reading of the system. `execution_debt`: `none` when the two figures are the same, or `pending_verifier` and then the cases nobody has executed yet, with who owes each execution and since when. That debt is not a gate of its own: what this step asks you for is the declaration and not a figure above zero, because the method cannot make a person execute. But it is never silent either, and a step that advances with its debt declared is honest in a way that a step advancing while it looks complete is not. And walk the screens yourself, which is a different thing from the execution a person owes and never a replacement for it: you walk to find defects, a person executes to accept. Go through every screen the system has as a person would -- open it, look at what is on it, use it, and follow the flow from one screen to the next -- and write `docs/SCREEN_WALKTHROUGH.md` with `screens_total`, how many screens the system has; `screens_walked`, how many of them you actually walked; and `walkthrough_findings`, how many defects the walk found. A screen you could not reach is counted in the total and named with its reason: taking it out of the denominator so that the figure reads better is the one thing this file must never do. For every defect: the screen, what you saw, what you expected instead, and the pending item you opened for it. When you need a decision from me, ask it with the four parts of UX-28 (`docs/ANEXO_HEURISTICAS.md`) and in this order: the data that makes the question necessary, listed; one paragraph of at most five lines that links that data and lets the question be seen; the question itself with its possible answers numbered; and your recommendation. Say it at the technical depth the project declared in `communication_depth` -- `beginner`, `decision_maker` or `technical`; if the project declared none, use `decision_maker`.

## Prompt 2

For every case that passed, nothing. Then tell me, flow by flow, how many cases passed and how many failed, compared with the previous execution, and which failures repeat. If a case could not be executed, tell me why and whether the case or the system is the problem. Then refresh the three declarations at the head of the file: `cases_written`, `cases_executed_by_a_person` counted from the signed execution tables and from nothing else, and `execution_debt` with who still owes which execution and since when. And for every case that failed and whose failure was then fixed, the case does not count as verified again until a person runs it WHOLE and signs a new execution row later than the fix, written `rerun_after_a_fix` with the pending item that caused it. An execution row reports how far the person got: the steps it did not reach neither passed nor failed, so closing on the row that failed confuses not having seen it fail with having seen it work (Annex H, CQ-21.3). List the cases that owe a re-run, with the correction that fixed them and since when.
